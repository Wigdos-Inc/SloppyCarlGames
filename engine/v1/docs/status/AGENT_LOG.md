- [2026-08-20] MAIN: analysis only, no code changed — traced the texture-animation cost properly and **corrected its own earlier attribution from the same day**. MAIN had told the user the per-cycle noise bake was the freeze; the arithmetic says otherwise. For the stage0 water top `speck` resolves to 12px, so `speckCount` is `floor(240×160×2.0/144)` = ~533 — roughly 1200 canvas ops every ~2.5s, sub-millisecond, nowhere near the 16000 cap. The actual cost is per-frame: [`blendTextureSurfaces`](engine/v1/handlers/game/Texture.js#L31) does a `clearRect` plus two full-canvas `drawImage` calls, then `dirty` drives a full [`texImage2D`](engine/v1/handlers/Render.js#L1096) — a texture *reallocation*, not an update. stage0 carries three animated entries (`sea-surface` on the per-face path at 240×160, `energy-core` in two colour variants at 64×64 on the registry path — the only two `animation.able` presets), totalling ~186KB of reallocation per frame to convey a single float. `holdTime: 0` means there is no hold phase, so it never pauses. That matches the user's recollection of the engine freezing once two textures animated in the same frame, and points at a synchronization stall rather than a throughput problem. On the user's direct question of whether a GPU crossfade is genuinely cheaper or merely moves the cost: it is a change of complexity class, not a transfer — CPU compositing is O(texture area) per frame regardless of screen coverage (a 500×500 CNU surface at `textureScale` 2 is a million texels and 4MB uploaded *per frame*, ~240MB/s for one object), while a shader `mix()` is O(screen coverage), bounded by resolution, with upload reduced to once per bake cycle. The extra per-fragment fetch is real and peaks when the surface fills the screen, but estimates at 1–2% of a weak integrated GPU's texture throughput. Flagged as reasoned from source and bandwidth arithmetic rather than a profile. Also surfaced a latent scaling defect: `speckCount` is capped while its area term is not, so noise silently thins past ~1100×1100px. User accepted the framing; the work is active, not deferred, and scoped to four items — GPU crossfade, `texImage2D` → `texSubImage2D` on the dirty path, elapsed-time-budgeted bake amortization, and the uncapped-area/capped-`speckCount` defect. Items 1–3 are not redundant: at large authored sizes a bake still lands as one multi-megabyte upload and one full speck loop in a single frame even with the crossfade in place. Seam decided by the user: one animated-texture shader variant for general use across object types, not a water-specific path. Two approaches were rejected on principle and must not be re-proposed — retuning `sea-surface`'s `holdTime` (changes config, fixes nothing for a game developer, and papers over the engine's problem on the most important visual in an underwater game), and clamping per-face canvas dimensions (the engine's former behaviour: it did prevent the cost, but large faces then rendered tiled at the clamped size and looked, per the user, genuinely abysmal). Standing principle stated by the user: the engine must not be constrained to only produce something ugly, and current testGame usage does not bound performance scope — a developer could author a 500×500 CNU animated surface and the engine should handle it. MAIN also logged a speculative DEFERRED entry for this work and attributed it to the user, who had not deferred anything; deleted on correction
