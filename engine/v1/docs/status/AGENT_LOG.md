Running log of notable engine-touching work — not custom agents alone. Valid entry
sources are ERA, DRYAD, ARGUS, RIGOR, ED, SAGE, and MAIN (the main/orchestrating
agent acting directly, without spawning a custom agent). SAGE is responsible for
appending an entry here after any of these produces a meaningful finding or change,
including the main agent's own passes. The weekly status task reads this file and
then empties it (back to this header) after summarizing — so entries only need to
cover "since the last weekly report."

- [2026-09-21] MAIN: diagnosed and fixed two decal faults in the GLB export, found by the author's first Blender inspection of an exported Carl — stomach rendering as a black mess, mouth decal absent. Both were in `buildDecalVertices`, both from the same shortcut: the z-fight offset used a **fixed magnitude** and took its **direction from the triangle winding**. `node --check` passes; not yet author-verified.
  - **MAIN's first diagnosis was wrong** and the author's correction killed it. MAIN guessed a texture-to-primitive mix-up — that the mouth had been bound to the body's decal and the "lattice" on the belly was Carl's teeth. The author reported there were no teeth anywhere, and that `pattern.png`'s swirls were correctly positioned but only partly visible. That reframed it from a binding problem to a depth problem.
  - **Fault one — a fixed offset cannot clear the chord dip.** The decal is a 12×12 grid whose vertices are projected onto the host surface, so each flat triangle chords *inside* that surface between them. MAIN simulated the actual projected grid for every sphere-hosted decal on Carl and measured the max centroid dip: `body[2]` 0.00939, `body[1]` 0.00868, `body[0]` 0.00208 — all three above the 0.002 offset and buried, and they are the large circle decals forming the belly patch. `body[4]` (`pattern.png`) at 0.00175 and `head[0]` (`eyes.png`) at 0.00180 sit just *under* the threshold, which is exactly why the swirls rendered partially rather than not at all. The nose circles at 0.00001 were never at risk. The dip scales with the angle a decal spans, so no constant can be right.
  - **Fault two — a mirrored decal inverts the offset direction.** `mouth_close.png` is authored with `localTransform.scale` of `-0.25 x 0.125`. The negative x mirrors the quad, flipping triangle winding, which inverted the flat normal the offset was derived from — pushing the mouth *into* the snout. Its dip is 0.00017, twelve times under the threshold, so this was independent of fault one and is why the mouth vanished entirely rather than partially.
  - **One fix covers both.** Per triangle, re-project the centroid of its three already-projected vertices onto the host surface. That vector is simultaneously the exact local dip and the true outward direction. Offset by it plus a small epsilon along it. Flat hosts project to themselves, yielding a zero vector, so the direction falls back to the face axis read from column 2 of `decalFaceRotations`. The emitted NORMAL is flipped to agree with that direction. Nothing depends on winding any more and the magnitude adapts to any host size or decal span.
  - **The structural reason this class of bug exists,** worth knowing before decal geometry is touched again: the engine never hits it because it uses `POLYGON_OFFSET_FILL(-1,-1)` with `depthMask(false)` in a dedicated pass — depth-slope-scaled and winding-agnostic. glTF has no equivalent, so the exporter must solve analytically what the renderer gets free from the rasteriser.
  - **Red herring, recorded so it is not chased again:** the mouth is the only decal on Carl with `opacity` undefined, hence the only one whose registry `.source` is a raw `ImageBitmap` rather than a canvas. That looked like a perfect correlation with the only missing decal. It was coincidence — `encodeTextureImage`'s `CanvasImageSource` handling is fine.
  - **Superseded 2026-09-22.** The two root causes above still hold, but the fix described here was wrong and has been replaced — see the next entry.

- [2026-09-22] MAIN: replaced the decal lift fix from the previous entry, which was wrong. Its diagnosis of both root causes stood; its *granularity* did not. `node --check` passes; not yet author-verified.
  - **What gave it away.** The author re-exported and reported an artefact visible in Blender's **Layout tab under Solid shading**, which renders no materials at all. A visible artefact there is geometry, not texturing — that single observation invalidated the previous fix without needing any material inspection. The artefact was a black speckled rectangle, the signature of coincident self-intersecting surfaces.
  - **Why the previous fix failed.** It computed the lift magnitude per *triangle*, from that triangle's own centroid dip, and applied it along that triangle's own flat normal. Both magnitude and direction therefore differed between adjacent triangles sharing a corner, so the decal sheet was pulled apart into overlapping, mutually z-fighting shards instead of being displaced as one surface. The deeper the dip the worse the tearing, which is why the belly degraded rather than improved. Adaptive magnitude was right; per-triangle granularity was not.
  - **Replacement.** One scalar per decal — the deepest centroid dip across all its triangles, plus the epsilon — applied to every vertex along a new `decalSurfaceNormal`: the ellipsoid gradient for sphere, the xz gradient for cylinder, `faceAxis` for flat, and for capsule a unified form where inside the band the cap centre tracks the point's own y so the y term collapses into the cylinder normal. Both the scalar and the normal field are continuous functions of vertex position, so shared corners receive identical displacement and the sheet stays watertight. That same analytic normal is what the decal emits, which removes the winding dependence entirely — `triangleNormal` is now used only by `buildMeshVertices`.
  - **Verified offline.** Simulated the full projected grid for all thirteen sphere-hosted decals on Carl, applied the new rule, and measured every lifted triangle centroid against the host surface. All clear, with minimum clearance uniformly 0.0018–0.0020 whether the decal's dip was 0.00001 or 0.00939 — the lift compensates the dip exactly and leaves a consistent gap, which is precisely what the per-triangle version could not do.

- [2026-09-22] MAIN: third iteration on decal export. The previous whole-sheet lift **converted a z-fighting problem into a stacking-order problem**; replaced with a per-host uniform lift, explicit authored-order layering, and adaptive grid resolution. `node --check` passes; not yet author-verified.
  - **The regression.** The lift was computed per decal from that decal's *own* deepest dip. On Carl's body the two large circle decals dip ~0.009 and so lifted ~0.011, while `pattern.png` dips 0.0018 and lifted only 0.0037 — putting the dark circles physically **above** the swirls and occluding them, which is why they went from partly visible to completely gone. Physical height had become the layering rule. The engine never has this problem because all decals draw in one pass with `depthMask(false)`, blending in authored order with no height separation at all.
  - **Fix, three coordinated parts.** The lift is now uniform across a host's decals (deepest dip among them, plus epsilon), so every decal clears by the same base. `index * decalLayerStep` is then added so decal N always sits above decal N−1 regardless of relative dips, matching the engine's draw-order semantics. And `buildDecalGrid` now measures its dip at the base 12 segments and rebuilds once at `ceil(12 * sqrt(dip / target))` — capped at `decalMaxSegments` — since dip falls with the square of resolution. The third part is what makes the floating acceptable rather than merely correct.
  - **The author's complexity observation is right, and cuts the opposite way to intuition.** Host facets are chords *inside* the true surface, so the mathematical surface is an upper bound on the host mesh. A decal hugging the mathematical surface is therefore always above the host; one coarsened to match the host's own segment count would chord further inside and be strictly worse. Depth correctness wants decal geometry **finer** than the host it sits on, even though that costs more triangles than the surface beneath it.
  - **Verified offline** on Carl's body, the worst case. Clearance 0.00237; resolutions selected 25/32/32/23/23; dips after refinement 0.00048–0.00137, down from up to 0.00939. Lifts strictly increase with authored index (0.00237 → 0.00397), so `pattern.png` at index 4 now sits highest. All five clear, minimum clearance 0.00150–0.00349. Peak float drops from 0.0114 to 0.0040 on a 0.5 half-extent — 0.8% of radius against 2.3%.
  - `decalSurfaceOffset` dropped 0.002 → 0.001 now that adaptive resolution absorbs most of the dip. `buildDecalVertices` split into `buildDecalGrid` (projected grid, normals, UVs, measured dip) and a thin emitter taking a grid plus a lift scalar; cell iteration extracted to `forEachDecalCell`, shared by the dip measurement and the emit pass.
  - **Author confirmed this resolved the decal depth and layering faults.**

Everything verified clean: container length matches file size; 43 nodes, 72 materials/textures/images; 69 `BLEND` plus 3 `OPAQUE` (the base bakes); every image PNG colour type 6 RGBA; no `baseColorFactor` anywhere; and per-mesh primitive → material → texture mapping correct, each base primitive on its 64×64 repeat-sampler texture and each decal on its own clamp-sampler image.
  - **Alpha is genuinely correct**, not merely present: `pattern.png` 72.1% transparent, `eyes.png` 81.0%, `mouth_close.png` 62.2%, `suckers_4x2.png` 35.6%, and the circle shape decals 20.9% — which is 1 − π/4, exactly a circle inscribed in its square.
  - **Cause of the appearance.** `canvas.toBlob` writes RGB `(0,0,0)` wherever alpha is `0` — confirmed, every decal image reads `topLeftRGBA [0,0,0,0]`. Harmless to anything honouring alpha. The author was in Blender's **Texture Paint** mode, which paints the slot image straight onto the surface without compositing material alpha, so those texels render as raw black across the full rectangle. That uniformity across every texture is what ruled out the narrower explanation.
  - **Only shape decals are square, and that is the engine's own bake** — `compositeShapeDecal` always allocates 256×256. Image decals keep native aspect (370×265, 500×500, 677×369, 158×314). The exporter adds no padding.
  - **A proposed fix was rejected, and the reasoning is worth keeping.** MAIN proposed emitting `OPAQUE` instead of `BLEND` for decals whose bake is wholly opaque (the 27 square-mask tentacle decals). The author rejected it on two grounds, both correct: the accompanying comment was three lines of prose, violating the comment rule the author had *already* corrected MAIN on within this same feature; and a change touching 27 of 72 textures cannot explain a symptom reported as affecting all of them — MAIN had pattern-matched to the data it had just gathered rather than to what was actually reported.
  - **Confirmed 2026-09-22.** The author switched to Material Preview and all textures display correctly. The export was always correct; Texture Paint was the cause.
