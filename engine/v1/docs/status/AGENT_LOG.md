- [2026-09-02] MAIN: normalised primitive winding to outward across seven builders in `NewObject.js` — **browser-verified by the author.** Framed as a renderer prerequisite at first, but it is a **live physics bug on its own**. - [2026-09-02] MAIN: **root cause of the void stencil bug found, and it is architectural — the per-pixel stencil reject cannot express a depth-bounded cut.** Measured, not inferred: replaced the eyeball diagnostic with a numeric one (64 fullscreen quads writing the counter into the red channel, `readPixels`, histogram on `window.voidCounterResult`). - [2026-09-02] MAIN: fixed both aperture stencil symptoms — **reverted, see above.** Root cause: a cap coplanar with its host is a **front** face when viewed from outside and a **back** face when viewed from inside, and the two need opposite treatment — the front one must pass depth so it does not `DECR`, the back one must fail so it does `INCR`. A single shared `polygonOffset` cannot satisfy both, which is why `(-1, -1)` left the aperture closed from inside (back cap biased toward the camera, passed, never counted) and torn from outside (the *slope* term varies per triangle, so some cap triangles got enough bias to pass and others did not — the author's "triangles arbitrarily close/open"). Fix is two culled passes distinguished by **depth function** rather than by offset magnitude: back faces under `LESS` (equal depth fails, counts) with `polygonOffset(0, 2)`, front faces under `LEQUAL` (equal depth passes, does not cancel) with `polygonOffset(0, -2)`. No slope factor in either — that was the cause of the reverted attempt's screen-wide punch. Verified by driving the browser directly (teleport plus synthetic `ArrowLeft`/`ArrowRight` to steer the third-person yaw): from inside the `cave-tunnel` bore the mouth now shows sky instead of rock; from outside the aperture is open and holds shape across a camera nudge; no blackout with the camera inside a void; `gl.getError()` 0 throughout. - [2026-09-05] MAIN: found and fixed the cause of the author's friend reporting severe framedrops **only while walking with WASD** — jumping and standing still were both free. No agents spawned. **Root cause: [`computeWorldAabbFromGeometry`](engine/v1/builder/NewObject.js#L308) rebuilt the full model matrix once per vertex.** It called a module-local `transformPoint(localPoint, transform)` helper defined as `TransformPointByMatrix(localPoint, CreateModelMatrix(transform))` inside its sweep loop; `CreateModelMatrix` composes T, Rx, Ry, Rz and S and does four `MultiplyMatrix4` calls. The player model is 43 parts totalling **15,522 vertices**, so one full re-pose cost 15,522 matrix composes — roughly 78,000 Float array allocations and 4 million multiply-adds — where 43 would do. `runPhysicsLoop` calls `UpdateEntityModelFromTransform` up to six times per physics tick, and that reaches `refreshEntityDerivedState` → `applyModelPose` → `UpdateObjectWorldAabb` per part whenever the pose orientation changed. **Why it was movement-specific:** `shouldSkipCollisionPipeline` skips `runPhysicsLoop` entirely at zero displacement, so standing still never re-posed; airborne, orientation holds and `UpdateEntityModelFromTransform` takes the cheap translate-only branch, which is why jumping was free; walking on the ground changes orientation every tick — yaw tracks the movement direction and `ApplyPlayerSurfaceOrientation` writes pitch/roll from the ground normal — so every walking tick paid the full re-pose. The same defect sat in `computeTriangleSoupFromMesh`, whose `readVertex` closure composed a matrix per vertex read, three per triangle: 136 meshes in lvl2 stage0 carry triangle-soup detailed bounds totalling 9,902 triangles, so a rebuild did ~29,700 composes where 136 would do.
