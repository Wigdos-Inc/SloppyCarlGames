- [2026-09-11] MAIN: **author-confirmed — the `tryOBB` contact-normal change fully fixes the backwards lean, closing the last of the three original slope-physics defects.** Confirmed by playtest, not by instrumentation.
  **An engine property understood for the first time, surfaced by the author noticing that a foot over a ledge still counts as grounded while the narrowphase bounds are not touching.** `ProbeGroundContact`'s acceptance test is a true-distance check **in any direction** against `radius + groundSnapTolerance` — for the player, 0.51 + 0.01 = **0.52** — measured from the capsule's contact-cap centre. It is neither a downward test nor a point test: it is effectively a **sphere of radius 0.52 centred just above the feet**. Since the player's capsule radius is 0.51, that detection sphere is very nearly his own body width, so ground lying to the *side* of the cap centre grounds him. That is the whole mechanism behind the forgiving ledge behaviour.
  **Consequence:** the engine already has footprint-width ground detection, expressed as a sphere rather than as directional rays. The four-probe grounding design under discussion is therefore **largely redundant for grounding specifically**; its remaining value would be sampling orientation *across* the footprint rather than at one contact point, and the edge-contact normal fix removed the case that made that urgent. MAIN recommends parking that work — a recommendation, not a decision.
  **The same property, both faces:** this omnidirectional acceptance is precisely what made an end cap *behind* the player eligible as ground. The fix did not remove the omnidirectionality; it made the reported normal honest, so a surface detected behind the player can no longer tilt him backwards even though it is still detected.
  **Still open and undecided,** unchanged from the earlier entry today: above `MaxDeltaDegrees` the correction refuses the surface and calls `resetSurfaceState`, setting `playerState.grounded` false, but `runPhysicsLoop` overwrites `entity.grounded` from the raw probe hit regardless — so the player reads as grounded while untilted on a steep slope, and can still jump from it.

- [2026-09-11] MAIN: **the backwards-lean bug is an OBB edge-contact ambiguity, and it is fixed.** Every earlier theory was wrong: it was never face-selection-by-distance, never caused by the collider rotating, and never reachable by a coupling rule.
  **The measurement that settled it.** Sweeping the player's z from 392.2 to 392.9 across the failing band at the lip of the main slope, the probe is outside `main-slope-10` on **both** the box's up axis and its length axis at *every single sample*. The nearest feature is therefore always the **edge** where the top face meets the end cap, never a face interior. `tryOBB` still had to return one face normal and resolved that by largest axis excess — a tiebreak with no geometric meaning. The two excesses are close and cross over: at z 392.65 they are 0.333 and 0.386 so the end cap wins; at z 392.70 they are 0.372 and 0.349 so the top face wins. The end cap's normal is **(0, 0.866, −0.5)** — 30° from vertical, leaning back toward the platform while the player walks forward — and the top face's is (0, 0.5, 0.866) at 60°. So the reported normal flipped between a 30° backwards lean and a 60° forwards one under sub-centimetre movement. One cause, both symptoms.
  **The fix** is the standard sphere-vs-box result: the contact normal points from the closest point back to the probe. On a face interior that is arithmetically the face normal, so nothing on open ground changes; on an edge it is the direction the box actually pushes, and it can never point backwards because it always points from the surface toward the entity.
  **Why the main slope and never the ramps.** The slope is eleven rotated boxes. Rotating a box rotates its end faces into walkable range and creates edges between faces whose normals differ by 90°. Triangle-soup ramps present each surface as its own triangle with its own normal, so no single primitive has two faces to choose between.
  **An engine fact worth recording independently:** the ground probe is **not directional**. The probe *origin* rotates with the model, but `tryOBB` and `trySoup` are proximity queries — closest point, then distance within radius plus tolerance — with no direction in them at all. Only `tryAABB` is a genuine vertical drop. A surface behind and below the entity is fully eligible to be its ground. The author deduced this from a screenshot, reasoning that a probe angled with the model could not hit a face sitting behind the player.
  **Verification.** Across the failing band: **zero backwards normals**, where the same sweep previously produced the (0, 0.87, −0.50) cap normal at five of fifteen samples; the two samples still registering `main-slope-10` now report normal z of **+0.06 and +0.16**, forward-leaning. Walking off the platform edge slowly and at full speed: no backwards normal, no rotation, no launch — y at the edge 36.13, peak afterwards 36.13 slow and 36.14 fast. Landing from a 1.2-unit drop onto four slabs: all four ground, **20/20 frames after first contact, zero flips**, tilting to 18° and 30° where the allowance permits and staying flat at 48° and 60° where it does not. Regression: standing on all eleven slabs **11/11 at 20/20 frames, zero flips, 0.00° normal error**; all fifteen triangle-soup ramps clean; level gates unchanged at terrain 61 / obstacles 45 / entities 18 / terrain voids 38; console zero errors; frame pacing median 13.5 ms, max 14.6 ms, zero frames over 20 ms.
- [2026-09-11] MAIN: **prototyped and removed a single-point pose validation — recorded so it is not retried.** Ahead of finding the edge-contact cause, a cheap test of the author's iterative-refinement idea placed a check point one cap-height along each candidate's normal and rejected the candidate if its surface was no longer reachable from there. It **failed to reject the end cap**, because moving along a face's own normal keeps the check point over that same face — the test is close to tautological, measuring 0.432 against a 0.52 band at the lip. The candidate-list restructure was kept — behaviour-neutral, cost-neutral, and the scaffolding a real multi-probe validation would need.

- [2026-09-08] MAIN + ED + ERA + DRYAD: **`angle` deleted from the ramp primitives outright, and `ramp-complex` given an authorable curve** — implemented by ED to a written author-approved plan, audited by ERA and DRYAD in parallel, findings applied by MAIN.   **Why the field was deleted rather than redefined.** `resolveRampShape` computed `rise = tan(angle) * depth` for both ramp shapes. On `ramp-simple` that *is* the surface incline and was correct. On `ramp-complex` the surface is a quarter-cosine climbing to that rise, so `angle` actually described the **chord** while the real surface was steeper past the middle — authored 35° produced a 47.7° crest, authored 45° produced 57.5°. Separately the curve's shape was not authorable at all: its only lever was `dimensions.y`, so tuning the curve meant changing the object's height, which desyncs a ramp from the geometry around it. **The resolution came from noticing the field was redundant, not merely wrong.** A ramp runs corner to corner — toe at bottom front, crest at top back — so its incline is already fully described by `dimensions.y` and `dimensions.z` together. The clincher: every existing `ramp-simple` in the project authored `angle` in exact agreement with its box — `slope-fixed-ramp-15` is 20 deep with `dimensions.y` 5.359, and `tan(15°) × 20` is 5.359 exactly, holding identically at 25/35/45/55. The field never carried information the box did not already have.
  **The contract now.** `dimensions.y` is the exact height on both shapes, `dimensions.z` the exact footprint, incline is derived and never authored, and `depth` (`ramp-complex` only, 0–1, default 0.5) controls how hard the curve sags between the two fixed corners — 0 the straight chord, 1 tangent to the bottom face at the toe and vertical at the crest. **Every `depth` shares the same two endpoints**, so a ramp always fills its box and no authored combination can produce an impossible shape — which is why the new code contains no clamp and no rejection anywhere.
  **Rejected alternatives, all live decisions at the time.** *Redefining `angle` as the maximum incline* was designed in full and dropped: with depth, height and max incline all authored and all honoured, the curve shape is already determined and a rate parameter has nothing left to choose — it would have needed a flat-run slack concept whose usable range varied with the box. *A power-law profile* `y = H·(z/D)^k` with `k = 1/(1-depth)` reaches a literal two-flat-faces shape at `depth` 1, but its exponent goes infinite there and `Math.pow(1, Infinity)` is `NaN`, so that endpoint would have needed writing out separately; the Bézier is finite across the whole range. *A general Bézier evaluator* — more code for one caller. *Splitting the curved top face group per segment* — the author ruled that a flatter or steeper curve is the same kind of orientation variation the single chord normal already spans, and splitting would demand the same treatment at every intermediate angle.
  **Geometry.** Quadratic Bézier with `P0` at the toe, `P2` at the crest, `P1` lerped from the chord midpoint toward the bottom-back corner by `depth`. Reduced forms used directly: `z(t) = halfDepth·((2t-1) + 2·depth·t·(1-t))` and `y(t) = baseY + rise·((1-t)·t·(1-depth) + t²)`. `dz/dt = 2·halfDepth·(1 + depth·(1-2t))` is non-negative across the range, reaching zero only at `t=1, depth=1` where the crest is vertical by design, so the surface never folds back.
