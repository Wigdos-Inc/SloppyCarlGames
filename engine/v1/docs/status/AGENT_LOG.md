Running log of notable engine-touching work — not custom agents alone. Valid entry
sources are ERA, DRYAD, ARGUS, RIGOR, ED, SAGE, and MAIN (the main/orchestrating
agent acting directly, without spawning a custom agent). SAGE is responsible for

then empties it (back to this header) after summarizing — so entries only need to

- [2026-09-24] MAIN + ED + ERA + DRYAD: replaced the single decal-centred surface projection with a per-shape one, and added a `pill` shape decal primitive — both authorized by the author in the same breath ("worth going both for the cap-fix, and the new primitive"). Shipped and independently verified; two real bugs were found in the GLSL half *after* ED and both audits had signed off.
  - **The defect being fixed was worse than the previous pass recorded, and differently shaped.** That pass logged it as "`u`'s slope kinks from −1.145 to −20.878 across the tube/cap seam, an eighteen-fold change in rate." Re-measured properly, `du/d(arc)` is *continuous* across the seam (ratio 1.000 either side); that characterisation was wrong in form and is superseded. The real defect was a pervasive horizontal stretch that grew with vertical distance from the decal centre and then folded on the cap. On a tentacle-sized capsule the old map drew artwork 2.24× too wide at mid-tube, 3.80× just below the seam and 4.12× at the seam; beyond that, `dot(n, centreDir)` goes negative and the map folds over itself, past 99×. Vertical scale was 1.00 throughout, which is why the symptom read as horizontal smearing rather than a tear.
  - **What replaced it.** Sphere: azimuthal equidistant about the decal centre. Cylinder, and capsule whose centre is in the axis band: an axis-anchored unroll whose sheet is (`rho * dTheta`, meridian arc), so the tube — a developable surface — unrolls with zero distortion and the cap keeps the tube's meridians straight. Capsule whose centre is on a cap: the cap *is* a sphere, so it takes the sphere branch about the cap centre. That last branch exists only so a decal on a capsule's `top`/`bottom` side is not degenerate under the axis unroll (`rho` would be zero); no content exercises it, and it was implemented on potential-usage grounds rather than current need. The antipodal-seam unwrap moved from `SelectDecalFacets` into `DecalSurfaceUv` behind a `referenceArc` parameter, because `u` now mixes `arc` with `rise` and the caller could no longer recompose it; it runs only on the axis-anchored branch, since on a sphere the antipodal discontinuity is a bearing flip rather than a 2π branch change and unwrapping there would corrupt it.
  - **Measured on the shipped code, by MAIN, independently of ED's report** — the functions were lifted out of `Render.js` by text and run against generated host geometry rather than trusting the hand-back. Decal centre maps to uv zero exactly; authored size preserved at the centre to 2.99e-10; footprint cross-term 1.32e-15, meaning a rectangle stays a rectangle where the old map sheared rotated off-axis decals into parallelograms by 11% (Carl's two 45° nostrils); zero coverage misses across 46,486 samples at margins 1.0 and 2.0; worst vertex-to-facet distance 8.67e-19, so the geometry still *is* the host surface. Artwork scale under the new map: 1.00 everywhere on the tube, 1.15 at 30° up the cap, 2.00 at 60°, 5.76 at 80° — the 1/cos(latitude) growth is the unavoidable price of keeping meridians straight, and Gauss rules out avoiding it entirely. Triangles on Carl's 67 curved decals rose 7,460 → 8,356 at margin 1.0, because decals now genuinely cover the footprint they were authored to cover instead of folding into a sliver; still far under the old fixed grid's 19,872.
  - **The author's actual stated goal was verified directly, not inferred.** For a decal authored half a circumference wide and centred on the tube, the old map put its `u = 0.5` edge at 90.000° on the tube but collapsed it to 0.009° the instant it reached the cap. Under the new map that edge is a dead-straight 90.000° meridian at every latitude tested, from mid-tube to 85° up the cap — the clean line covering exactly half the tube and half the hemisphere that the author described from the old Carl model.
  - **Two mirror bugs in the GLSL, shipped by ED and missed by both audits.** The GLSL cannot run in Node, so MAIN hand-transcribed `decalSurfaceUvGLSL` out of the file and diffed it against the shipped JS over 8,177 host samples. First: the `pi` literals were `3.14159265359` / `6.28318530718`, both slightly *larger* than `Math.PI`, so at the antipodal wrap boundary the two halves took different branches. Second: ED's `decalAxisAngle` returned `0.0` when `x` and `z` are both zero, reasoning that `Math.atan2(0, 0)` also returns 0 — but `Math.atan2` at the origin returns `0`, `π`, `-0` or `-π` depending on the *signed* zeros, and `buildCapsule` emits pole vertices whose `x` and `z` are negative zero, so the halves disagreed by π at every capsule pole vertex. Before: worst uv difference 1.29, twelve keep/discard disagreements. MAIN fixed both — full-precision literals, and `decalAxisAngle` now falls back to the decal centre's own angle so a pole vertex gets `dTheta = 0` and lands on the centre meridian, which is the geometrically meaningful convention rather than an arbitrary one, shared by both halves. After: worst uv difference 8.88e-16, one double ulp, zero keep/discard disagreements. **The lesson is that a JS/GLSL mirror pair needs a mirror test, not two code reviews** — neither audit was equipped to catch either bug by reading.
  - **MAIN's own methodological error, recorded because it nearly produced a false finding.** The first four validation runs reported size errors of 6.12e-3, 5.59e-6 then 1.85e-6, and MAIN initially attributed each to the mapping. Every one was the probe: a non-orthogonal frame in the test, then a spherical probe step used where a tube's meridian is a straight vertical line, then one-sided finite-difference truncation. The true figure, 2.99e-10, appeared only once the probe followed the real surface. What identified each in turn was the residual's scaling in `h` — an error that *shrinks* as `h` grows is roundoff, one that grows as `h²` is truncation, and one that is flat in `h` is neither and therefore structural.

  - **Fault two — a mirrored decal inverts the offset direction.** `mouth_close.png` is authored with `localTransform.scale` of `-0.25 x 0.125`.   - **One fix covers both.** Per triangle, re-project the centroid of its three already-projected vertices onto the host surface. That vector is simultaneously the exact local dip and the true outward direction. Offset by it plus a small epsilon along it. Flat hosts project to themselves, yielding a zero vector, so the direction falls back to the face axis read from column 2 of `decalFaceRotations`. The emitted NORMAL is flipped to agree with that direction. Nothing depends on winding any more and the magnitude adapts to any host size or decal span.
  - **The structural reason this class of bug exists,** worth knowing before decal geometry is touched again: the engine never hits it because it uses `POLYGON_OFFSET_FILL(-1,-1)` with `depthMask(false)` in a dedicated pass — depth-slope-scaled and winding-agnostic. glTF has no equivalent, so the exporter must solve analytically what the renderer gets free from the rasteriser.

- [2026-09-23] MAIN: settled the decal approach by measurement after the author corrected the goal — conforming geometry wins on both correctness and cost. Design conclusion only, no code changed.
  - **The author corrected the framing, and the correction is the important part.** MAIN had offered "revert to the old behaviour" as the safe option. It is not a baseline, it is the defect: decals were drawn on the *analytic* surface, so they **floated** above the faceted host, and a low-complexity host got perfectly smooth decal curves over sharp faceted geometry. "Not as bad as it used to be" does not justify a regression.
  - **Goal as confirmed by the author:** a decal must look like part of the host surface — same facets, same complexity, no float, no extra smoothness. `complexity` must be authoritative for decals, not bypassed by them. Fewer triangles is a **byproduct** of doing this correctly, never something to trade against it.
  - **Conclusion: build decal geometry from the host's own facets** — clip the host triangles to the decal footprint in decal space and draw exactly those. The decal is then not projected onto the host, it *is* the host surface, so conformance is structural rather than approximate. Measured on Carl's 69 decals: old fixed 12×12 grid **19,872** triangles and incorrect; MAIN's shipped shared matched grids **4,318** and incorrect (sinking up to 11% of host radius); conforming **2,318** and correct — an 88% reduction, worst single decal 119 triangles, and **zero decals exceeding the old 288**. Build to a 2× footprint margin — measured 2,524 against 2,318, only 9% more and still 87% below old, zero decals over 288 — then compute the decal UV **per-fragment** from the interpolated part-local position and the placement matrix, discarding outside `[0,1]`. Animation then changes only a uniform; geometry is never rebuilt at runtime, by the builder or anything else.
  - **What follows from the design.** The vertex shader gets *simpler* — `u_partWorld * position`, no projection, no snap — so it is cheaper per vertex than today. Depth becomes correct by construction: geometry exactly coincident with host facets is precisely the case `POLYGON_OFFSET_FILL` exists for, unlike the sinking geometry that shipped, which polygon offset was never going to rescue. The shared VAO is replaced on its own terms — instead of one 288-triangle buffer serving every decal, each decal holds only the facets it covers, median well under 34 triangles, built once. (2) Per-fragment UV replaces per-vertex projection — fragment count is unchanged since screen area is the same, but ALU per fragment rises; a net win is expected given 87% fewer vertices and triangles, and it has **not** been measured. (3) Build-time clipping of roughly 350 host triangles per decal at level build — expected trivial, but it is real work that did not exist before.

The margin plus per-fragment cut removes the rebuild entirely, and the measured cost is *lower* than the shared-grid alternative, the opposite of what MAIN asserted at the time. MAIN twice argued from an unmeasured cost assumption and was wrong in both directions; the measurements should have come first.

  - **Root cause.** The mapping measured its two angles against the placement's own u/v axes, which lie in the host's *face plane*. That is correct only for a decal authored at the face centre, where those axes are perpendicular to the direction from the ray origin to the decal. Offset the decal and that direction tilts, the axes are no longer perpendicular to it, and the measured angle is nonzero **at the decal's own centre** — so the decal cannot draw where it was authored. The only strip surviving the cut is where the angle happens to be zero, the face's centre line, which is exactly where both irises and both pupils ended up.
  - **Measured on the head, before the fix.** `eyeWhites` is authored at `x = 0` and mapped its centre to `u = 0.000`, correct. `leftIris`/`rightIris` at `x = ∓0.092` mapped theirs to `u = ∓1.452`; `leftPupil`/`rightPupil` to `∓2.905`. With the footprint at `|u| ≤ 0.5`, none of them could render at their authored position. **6 of 67** curved decals had their centre fall outside their own footprint — the four eye decals and the two nostrils. Tentacle decals were unaffected, since for a side-face decal on a capsule the u axis is already perpendicular to the centre direction; that is why only the face looked broken.
  - **Fix.** Re-orthogonalise u and v against the centre direction before measuring — `right = normalize(u − centreDir·dot(u, centreDir))`, likewise `up`. At the decal centre the surface normal equals `centreDir`, so `dot(centreDir, right)` is zero by construction and the angle is exactly zero. Applied to [`DecalSurfaceUv`](engine/v1/handlers/Render.js#L551) and its [GLSL mirror](engine/v1/handlers/Render.js#L501). Verified across all 67: worst `|u|` at a decal's own centre is now **0.000000**, down from 2.905.
  - **Pattern, not an incident — worth recording.** This is the **fourth** defect MAIN introduced into this one mapping in a single pass: the mean-radius reference that mis-sized decals on non-round hosts; the seam-unwrap period computed from a different radius than the arc; the span mismatch between facet selection and the fragment cut; and now the non-orthogonal frame. All four share one cause — a formula derived and shipped without asserting its invariants. The invariants are cheap and obvious in hindsight: *a decal's own centre must map to uv zero*, *a tiny decal must not change size*, *selection and cut must use identical numbers*. Each is one arithmetic check that would have caught the fault before the author saw it. The validation discipline MAIN applied to the facet clipper — which has held up perfectly across every round — was simply never applied to the mapping feeding it.

- [2026-09-23] MAIN: discussion pass on decal distortion raised by the author. No edits authorized or made. Two findings — one a genuine bug, one a measurably poor choice.
  - **Finding 1 — the mapping tears where a capsule's tube meets its cap.** On the tube `rise` is a plain height difference; on the cap it is `radius × angle`, measured from scratch with no accumulation of the distance already travelled. Measured on a tentacle-sized capsule (half extents 0.06 / 0.30 / 0.06, tube half-length 0.240): `rise` reads **0.23990 just below the seam and 0.00010 just above it** — a jump of the entire tube half-length. Slope matches at 1.000 either side, so it is purely a missing offset, not a scaling error. Confirmed structurally against source: tube `rise` reaches 0.24 while cap `rise` cannot exceed `radius × π` ≈ 0.19, so the two are on different scales and cannot join. The artwork restarts at the seam, which accounts for most of the "bends completely out of shape" the author reports. Fix direction: accumulate the tube distance before adding the cap's arc.
  - **Finding 2 — the projection is the worst of the available options, and MAIN chose it.** Measuring two angles independently shears the footprint's corners. Shape distortion (ratio of the Jacobian's singular values; 1.00 = artwork keeps its proportions) on a cap of radius 0.06, at 30/45/60/75/90° from the decal centre — **current: 1.15, 1.41, 2.00, 3.86, unbounded. Azimuthal equidistant: 1.05, 1.11, 1.21, 1.36, 1.57. Stereographic: 1.00 throughout** (conformal), at the cost of area reaching 4× by 90°. Gnomonic (the pre-change behaviour) matches the current mapping's shape distortion but with catastrophic area growth — 8× by 60°, 57× by 75°.
  - **Equidistant is a strict improvement**: it caps worst-case shape distortion at 1.57× instead of unbounded *and* preserves the reach-equals-size semantics the author asked for, since true distance from the decal centre is its defining property. The independent-angles construction was a naive choice with no compensating advantage.
  - **On square decals and curves, for the record:** a sphere cannot be flattened without distortion (Gauss), so a square decal must bow somewhere on a curved host. That is mathematics, not an engine limitation; only *where* the error goes is selectable. Gnomonic alone keeps straight edges straight, and its price is exactly the squashing and the 90° wall the author asked to remove. Equidistant preserves size with gently bowed edges. Stereographic preserves local shape — circles stay circular, corners stay square — at the price of artwork growing with reach.
  - **Applied to the stomach.** `bellyOpen` is authored 0.85 wide on a body sphere of half-extent 0.5, so it reaches ~49° from its centre: roughly **1.45× edge distortion today against ~1.12× under equidistant**. MAIN's position to the author: fix the seam and switch the projection first, since the stomach may stop being a problem without any special approach; choosing stereographic instead is a judgement about whether size or shape fidelity matters more for that artwork, and is not one to make sight-unseen.
  - Nothing decided. Still open: the zero-scale floor at validate/normalize, the glTF export border smear, and whether to re-author the tentacle sucker scales. ARGUS still not run.

- [2026-09-23] MAIN: second discussion pass on decal authoring, author-raised. No edits authorized or made. Two design conclusions, one verified by measurement.
  - **Tentacles — an axis-aligned unwrap gives exactly the old behaviour.** The author's requirement is a clean line at the halfway point continuing over the rounded end. That line is a *meridian of the tentacle*, so the measurement must anchor to the capsule's own axis; every projection considered so far measures from the **decal's** centre and is structurally incapable of putting an edge on a meridian.
  - **Measured** on a tentacle-sized capsule (half extents 0.06 / 0.30 / 0.06), with `u = capRadius × azimuth about the axis` and `v = meridian arc accumulated through the seam`: the tube/cap seam is **continuous** (jump 2e-7, slopes 1.0000 either side — no kink), which also eliminates the tear found in the previous pass; a half-wrap decal's boundary sits at **exactly 90° azimuth at every height**, tube and cap alike, since `u` depends only on azimuth, so the edge is one meridian; and shape distortion along the tube is **exactly 1.000**, a cylinder being developable.
  - **Its cost, measured with corrected normalisation:** horizontal stretch on the cap of 1.03 / 1.15 / 1.51 / 2.29 / 5.03 at 25 / 50 / 75 / 90 / 98% up, unbounded at the tip where meridians converge. Artwork stays honest over the tube and most of the cap, smearing only in the final sliver.
  - **Correction recorded.** MAIN's first attempt at that cap measurement reported 1.00 everywhere, which was wrong — the meridian step had been normalised by change in *height* rather than actual surface distance, cancelling the very quantity being measured. Caught and redone before reporting.
  - **Design implication:** the projection should depend on the host shape rather than being uniform. Cylinders and capsules have an axis to anchor to and want the axis-aligned unwrap; a sphere has no preferred axis, so a decal-centred projection remains right there.
  - **Stomach — the seams are structural, not an authoring error.** Each decal is projected from its own centre. On a flat surface nothing distorts, so every piece lands identically and shapes butt together perfectly; on a curve, two adjacent decals with different centres distort differently *exactly where they meet*, so the seam is unclosable regardless of placement care. That is why flat-surface intuition fails, and why two circles plus a square cannot be lined up.
  - **So: one decal, not several.** Available today with no engine change — author the stomach as an **image decal** with the silhouette and transparency drawn in; Carl already uses 30 image decals, so the path is proven, and one decal means one projection with nothing to align. Engine-side alternative — shape decals are limited to `square`/`circle`/`triangle` ([canonSchemas.json:1621](engine/v1/core/canonSchemas.json#L1621)), so a stadium/rounded-rectangle primitive would give the shape procedurally, which matters specifically if the stomach must stay tintable at runtime.
  - **Raised but explicitly not recommended, being unmeasured:** give each *host* one shared surface parameterisation that all its decals use, rather than each decal carrying its own. Multi-decal compositions would then align by construction, as ordinary texture mapping does. Stated tradeoff is that decals far from that map's equator distort more.
  - **Also noted:** the square "widening unpredictably" is substantially the current projection's fault, not an inherent property of square decals — it shears 2.00× at 60° where azimuthal equidistant holds 1.21×. A square may behave predictably once that is fixed, which could make two-circles-plus-a-square viable again.
  - Nothing decided. Open: the seam tear, the projection choice, the zero-scale floor, the glTF export border smear, and whether to re-author the tentacle sucker scales. ARGUS still not run.
