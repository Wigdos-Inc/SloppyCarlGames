- [2026-09-14] MAIN: **three small follow-up fixes to the surface-relative input pass, handled directly — no agent spawned, and not yet ARGUS-verified.** All three sit below the ED threshold: a `Math.max`, a `ResolveVector3Axis` wrap, and a one-line event-field swap in testGame.
  **`support = up.y` did not merely vanish past vertical — it inverted, and ran the decelerators backwards.** The deferred entry for this described it as disabling braking on a vertical wall, which understated it. [`ApplyDeceleration`](engine/v1/math/Collision.js#L611) computes `newSpeed = Math.max(0, speed - deceleration * dt)`, so a **negative** deceleration *increases* speed. `support` was raw `up.y`, which is `cos(incline)` and goes negative past 90°, so on any overhang or ceiling both call sites in `UpdateMovement` — the reverse-intent braking burst and the no-input decay — acted as accelerators. **MAIN initially reported this as "the player accelerates on an overhang" and the author corrected it from observation — no speed was gained, momentum was simply lost too slowly, and he correctly guessed gravity was outrunning the term.** Running the numbers confirms him: Carl's `deceleration` is 10 against `Gravity.Strength` 10, the spurious gain is `deceleration·|cos(incline)|` while the opposing pull is gravity's tangential component `g·sin(incline)`, so gravity dominates everywhere below **135°** and the net is still deceleration — just far too little. Only past 135° would the inverted term win outright. It also never fires while input is held with no reverse intent, which is the whole of the 450-frame loop run. On a vertical wall, the case the author actually noticed, there is no inversion at all: `up.y` is exactly 0, friction is exactly 0, and momentum bleeds purely to a tangential gravity that is at its maximum there. **The standing lesson is that the sign of a term is not its observable effect; MAIN stated a runtime outcome it had not measured, against an author who had.** Fixed with a new module constant `minSupport = 0.25` and `support = Math.max(Math.abs(up.y), minSupport)`: `Math.abs` removes the inversion, the floor restores braking authority on a wall. **0.25 was chosen to engage only past 75.5°** — the halfpipe peaks at 69.6° and is untouched, as is every ordinary slope, which is what the deferred entry's warning required, since the `decel·cosθ` against `g·sinθ` relationship is tuned and must not move on normal geometry. The floor is the knob if wall braking feels wrong in playtest.
  **`getPrimaryOppositeHeld` compared two vectors of different lengths to pick the dominant axis.** The function gates the skid burst by deciding whether the player holds the opposite of his travel direction, and it picks forward-versus-right by comparing `|dot(velocityDirection, camFwd)|` against `|dot(velocityDirection, camRight)|`. But `camRight` comes from `cross(forward, WORLD_NORMALS.Up)` at [Camera.js:475](engine/v1/handlers/game/Camera.js#L475), which is already horizontal and normalized — flattening it is a no-op and it is unit — while `camFwd` was the camera forward with `y` set to 0, giving length `cos(pitch)`. The comparison was rigged toward "right" by that factor: 0.966 at the default −15° pitch, 0.5 at 60°, and **0.0175 at the freecam pitch clamp of 89°**, where it reads "strafing" essentially always. The consequence is the skid testing the wrong input axis for a reversal. Fixed by wrapping `camFwd` in `ResolveVector3Axis` at its hoist site.
  **This revises correction #9 of the approved plan**, which said explicitly *"Do not normalize `camFwd`"* on the grounds that the seed needs it raw in both terms. Re-checked, and normalizing is not just safe but **invariant** for every consumer that was working: `cameraYaw = atan2(camFwd.x, camFwd.z)` is unaffected by magnitude, and the seed `ProjectOntoPlane(camFwd, up) + uphill·(1−|up.y|)·−dot(camFwd, up)` is linear in `camFwd` in *both* terms with the result passing through `ResolveVector3Axis`, so a uniform rescale divides straight out — the plan itself observed that "the ratio survives normalization" and then drew the more conservative conclusion. `getPrimaryOppositeHeld` is the sole consumer that is **not** scale-invariant, precisely because it compares a `camFwd` dot against a `camRight` dot. No new degeneracy: both camera modes clamp pitch (freecam ±89°, defaultcam `cfg.minPitch`/`maxPitch`), so the flattened forward never approaches the EPSILON clamp.
  **The testGame spark burst was silently dead and is now closed.** `testGame/main.js:590` tested `detail.groundContact === true`, a field removed by the collision-event reshape; [physics/Master.js:100](engine/v1/physics/Master.js#L100) now publishes `collision: { result, normal, approach, surface }` with `result` one of `"floor"`, `"wall"`, `"floor+wall"` or `"solid"`. Changed to `detail.collision.result.includes("floor")`, preserving the original intent of firing on any contact involving the floor rather than only a pure floor hit. This closes the "author-side, still outstanding" note carried in the previous entry. testGame is a game consumer, not an engine module, so MAIN handled it directly rather than routing it to an engine agent.
  **The `referenceNormal` latch was explained in full but not touched — it remains deferred.** Traced end to end for the author: [`ClassifySurface`](engine/v1/physics/Correction.js#L58) is a ratchet whose second test, `candidate.y >= referenceNormal.y && incline <= walkableLimit`, is what keeps a gradual climb walkable, because the reference follows the player up while contact holds. It is rewritten once per frame outside the correction loop at [physics/Master.js:147-148](engine/v1/physics/Master.js#L147) — to world up when `surfaceContact === "none"`, to the current surface normal when `"walkable"` and the position changed. **The trapdoor is that vertical is the first incline whose delta from world up exceeds *both* air limits** — `Ground` 35 and `Sliding` 75 ([config.js:124](engine/v1/core/config.js#L124)) — so `ClassifySurface` returns `"wall"`, `pushHit` ([Collision.js:1021](engine/v1/physics/Collision.js#L1021)) discards the hit, `resetSurfaceState` runs, and the reference stays world up: self-latching, since every later frame classifies identically. Every other incline has an escape — a 60° face measures delta 60 from world up, within `Sliding`, so it degrades to sliding and recovers down the ladder. Only vertical has no floor under the trapdoor. The author's two measured symptoms are the same mechanism from inside and outside: 117 consecutive `"none"` frames with the probe at exactly the rest distance, and coming to rest stationary in `"sliding"` on a 61.4° face he had just walked up, recovering only after backing down to 17.6°.

- [2026-09-14] MAIN + Plan agent + ED + ERA + DRYAD + ARGUS: **two distinct defects at exactly 90°, both author-confirmed fixed in playtest — an orientation singularity and a movement-input rank collapse.** They presented as one bug and were separated only after the author corrected MAIN three times.
  **The diagnosis arc is the most important thing here, because MAIN got it wrong twice.** The first ARGUS run tested `cave-tunnel` — the wrong geometry entirely; the author rejected all of it. The second reached the right tube but teleported the player and wrote `velocity` and `referenceNormal` directly, which destroyed the gradually-built reference that *is* the mechanism under test, making the climb look impossible when it actually works. The author rejected that too: *"I reject all your findings until you do an actual test that doesn't cheat its way to conclusions."* Only the third run — input-only, from a walkable start the author positioned by hand — produced the real result. The standing lesson: for anything involving `referenceNormal` or the carried input frame, setup may place the player once and must then drive with `Input` alone; writing the state under test invalidates the run.
  **Defect 1 — `computeAlignmentAngles` was singular at exactly 90°.** It solved pitch and roll with yaw held frozen (its JSDoc said so), and freezing yaw makes the solve singular wherever the target up-vector goes horizontal, because `cos(roll)·cos(pitch) = n.y`. Worst pitch change per 0.1° of yaw: 88.2° → 3.2, 89.9° → 45, **90.0° → 159.8**, 90.1° → 45, 105° → 0.4 — a spike centred exactly on 90. `uw-tube-winding` ends in a shaft whose lining is 300 of 300 triangles at exactly 90.0°. ARGUS caught the player correctly grounded there (probe distance exactly 0.51000, velocity exactly tangent) while `rotation.x` thrashed `-90, 95.8, ..., 48.6, -57, ..., -108.4, -90, +90, -90, +90, 0`; at speed one flip injected **4.868** of outward velocity in a single frame and ejected him. Fixed by solving all three angles from one basis. The YXZ formulas already existed inline in `ComposeEulerRotations`; extracting them was verified **bit-identical over 400,000 samples, 200,177 of them in the pole branch**. Post-fix: 0 pitch flips over 100° in both runs, and frames walkable at 90° went 9 → 119 (fast) and 63 → 150 (slow).
  **ED overrode two of MAIN's instructions and was right both times, on its own measurements.** MAIN's prescribed construction — re-flatten the facing each frame and project it — is not idempotent once the solve's own yaw feeds back: the decomposed yaw is the compass of the *tilted* forward, so re-flattening rotates the heading again, and the surface contour line is an attractor. ED measured **89° of heading rotation in 2 seconds standing still**, on every slope from 20° up, and irreversible. Steering the carried forward instead gives 1.1e-14 drift over 300 frames and converges to *exactly* the old solve under a steady driven yaw. Second, MAIN's prescribed fallback (the entity's existing forward axis) collapses on the Level 2 entry case: running head-on into a vertical wall with a flat body, that axis *is* the flattened facing, so both candidates vanish and the basis comes out all zeros — verified up-vector error 1.414, a 90° miss.
  **Defect 2 — the movement side was a rank collapse, and it is correctness, not design.** The author isolated it: *"His rotation was stable, but the movement wasn't."* `getMovementDirection` applied **two** projections in sequence — flatten the camera basis to world XZ, then project onto the surface plane. The image of one plane projected onto another is 2D *unless the planes are perpendicular*, which is exactly `alignedUp.y == 0`. Algebraically `ProjectOntoPlane(v, n).y = v.y − n.y·(v·n)`, and with `v.y = 0` from the flattening that is identically zero when `n.y` is zero. Measured: on 144 frames of vertical wall with forward held and boost on, **vertical velocity increased on 0 of them**, max single-frame gain 0.0000, decaying 12.483 → −1.349, while circumferential velocity swung −11.098 → +8.874 with 16 azimuth sign flips. The player could not climb, and the 25 u/s² he applied trying went entirely sideways — that was the swerve. It also degrades continuously well before vertical: uphill authority scales with `up.y`, so the halfpipe at 69.6° already ran at ~35%.
  **The contract, and the design iteration that failed.** MAIN first proposed blending transport and camera authority live by `w`. The author refuted it concretely: halfway through a loop the player is not necessarily rotating the camera, so at a ceiling `w = 0` and the camera would reassert the original direction while the loop demands its opposite — and if the rules blend continuously there is no "rule switch" for the press event to gate. The resolution separates the two mechanisms: **`w` governs only the seed and never acts per frame**, while the held path evolves differentially by camera yaw delta composed with transport onto the current plane. That also closed a hole MAIN found in the author's literal wording — carrying the *direction* breaks flat-ground steering, because transport across a flat plane is the identity; carrying the *rule* is what he meant, and the differential form delivers both. The seed weight `w = 1 − |up.y|` is geometric rather than tuned: flat and ceiling are both horizontal planes where camera-XZ already lies in the surface plane, so both `w` and the un-normalized `|uphill| = sin(incline)` vanish at both ends and peak at vertical. The term `−dot(camFwd, up)` is exactly what the old projection computed and discarded.
  **Five hazards caught by the Plan agent before implementation, one of which would have shipped a permanent lockup.** (1) `alignedUp` is a *step* function — `resetSurfaceState` snaps it to world up the instant contact is lost — so leaving a wall while carrying ≈(0,1,0) makes `ProjectOntoPlane((0,1,0),(0,1,0))` the zero vector, `ResolveVector3Axis` returns zero **silently**, and every later frame projects zero: dead input until release-and-press. Degenerate transport therefore had to become a third re-seed trigger, which also covers `SetPosition` and respawn without enumerating them. (2) The degeneracy test must be an explicit magnitude check — `EPSILON` is 1e-6, so at `|proj| = 1e-4` normalization turns float noise into a unit vector. - [2026-09-12] MAIN + ED + ERA + DRYAD + ARGUS: **five follow-up fixes after the author playtested Pass 2 of the grounding rework — all author-confirmed in playtest.**
  **The headline: `surfaceContact` is now the single predicate for the sliding state.** The author reported having almost no control as though sliding, while slope correction still planted his feet, while he could not jump, while the model never went on its back — and hypothesised being marked grounded and sliding at once. The real state was a third combination: `surfaceContact` `"sliding"` with `action` `"Floating"`. Two facts combined. `ResolvePlayerState` had the underwater branch **above** the sliding branch, and the main slope below y 30 is underwater, so `action` was forced to `Floating` and the sliding branch never ran. Separately, `ApplySurfaceCorrection` sets `alignedUp` to the surface normal for **both** walkable and sliding — that branch keys on incline versus `FlatSnapDegrees`, not on contact — so the feet were planted regardless. Three consumers were reading three sources of truth: grounding read `surfaceContact`, movement damping read `grounded` + `surfaceContact`, orientation read `action`. Orientation was the odd one out and `action` is downstream of a machine that can preempt it. Fixed both ends — the back-rotation predicate became `surfaceContact === "sliding"`, and the sliding branch moved above the underwater branch so `action` follows rather than contradicts. This also removed a one-frame orientation lag ARGUS had found, since `surfaceContact` is written earlier in the same pipeline. The author's framing is worth keeping: *"Being on the ground with slope correction NEVER should induce Floating"*, and *"whether sliding is correct or not is not important if the system cannot even agree whether ground is ground."*
  **Ground snap tolerance was vertical and had to be perpendicular.** `restDeltaY` is `(radius / normal.y) - t`, a vertical distance, compared against a flat `groundSnapTolerance` of 0.01 — so the perpendicular error the snap could actually correct was `0.01 × normal.y`: 0.01 on flat, **0.005 at 60°**. Corrective authority shrank exactly as the slope steepened while the excursion to correct grew; past that window the player drifted perpendicular until the probe missed at ~0.52 and `surfaceContact` dropped to `"none"` — grounding lost with **no** slide, because `"none"` is not `"sliding"`. Now compares `Math.abs(deltaY) * normal.y`. Flat is unchanged (`normal.y` is exactly 1).
  **The sliding rotation sign was inverted.** ARGUS measured the model's forward vector against the surface normal and got exactly **−1** — face-down, not on his back. `Math.PI / 2` → `-Math.PI / 2`; re-verified at **+1**, pitch −33.2° on a 56.8° face.
  **Uphill input is damped during a slide.** The author could steer sideways and accelerate back *up* a slope. Input opposing the downslope direction now scales to 0.15; cross-slope and downhill keep full strength. The downslope vector is world down projected onto the surface via `ProjectOntoPlane` and is **deliberately not normalized** — only the sign of the dot matters, and leaving it un-normalized makes a flat surface yield exactly zero, so the comparison is false and the scale is 1. That is correct behaviour with no division by zero and no defensive guard, and the flat case is genuinely reachable because a slide keeps its frozen `referenceNormal` after running out onto flat ground. ARGUS: full uphill input with boost on a 57° face gained 0.0916 of height (all landing residual) while descending and accelerating 3.63 → 6.03. Steering survives but is faint — 0.287 units over 38 frames — and that weakness is `airControl` 0.2 applying to an ungrounded sliding player, not the new damping.
  **Angle limits are now authorable per medium.** The author observed that water is far denser, so a sliding player underwater ends up stationary, and that sliding underwater makes no physical sense — ground friction against water density would simply stop you unless the ground itself were unstable, which the engine does not model. MAIN first hardcoded `underwater ? limits.Sliding : …`; the author corrected that the values should be *data*. Final shape `MaxAngleDelta: { Air: { Ground: 35, Sliding: 60, Recover: 20 }, Water: { Ground: 60, Sliding: 60, Recover: 20 } }`, with a non-exported `angleLimitsFor(underwater)` selecting the table. Setting `Water.Ground` equal to `Water.Sliding` is what collapses the sliding band to nothing underwater — no gap remains for it to occupy — so a water slide band is now a data change. `Water.Recover` is inert at these values and exists so both tables share a shape. **The ground-snap gate had to read the same table**: had it kept reading a single global `Sliding` while classification used the water table, surfaces would classify walkable that the snap then refuses to seat the player on — structurally the same defect as the tolerance bug above.

Refuted; the reset was left untouched.
